### Where the Name Comes From

Two places, and I like that they turned out to agree.

The first is the red pen — the one an editor reaches for in a margin.

The second I only found out about afterwards. The [Penny Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Red) was a British postage stamp, issued in 1841 to replace the Penny Black and printed until 1879. The switch to red was practical rather than decorative. A Penny Black was cancelled with a red mark, and a red mark could be washed off, which let people reuse a stamp they had already posted. Printing the stamp red instead meant the Post Office could cancel it in black, where the mark showed plainly and could not be lifted off.

So the name belongs to a thing that was made red precisely so a correction would stay visible. That is the entire argument for the note at the foot of these posts, and I did not plan it.

The split is deliberate. Mechanical mistakes get corrected straight away: spelling, punctuation, agreement, American English. Anything that would change how I sound I decide on myself, and those decisions stay between us. Every correction Penny Red has caught is listed on the [copy feedback](/copy-feedback/) page with the rule behind it, so you can check the reasoning rather than take an AI's word for it.

We share the place with robots now, and I would rather be upfront about how I use AI than have you wonder.
